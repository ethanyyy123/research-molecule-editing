\begin{abstract}
Cognate redocking---reproducing a receptor's own deposited experimental
ligand model---is a standard quality-control step in structure-based virtual
screening. Two recent cryo-EM ATR structures (PDB~9L40, 2.87~\AA{},
berzosertib-bound; PDB~9L4B, 3.20~\AA{}, camonsertib-bound) were redocked
with AutoDock Vina (2.0~\AA{} symmetry-corrected RMSD pass threshold). 9L4B
passed (0.61$\pm$0.02~\AA{}) whereas the higher-resolution 9L40 failed
(2.53$\pm$0.01~\AA{}), not explained by the nearby allosteric pocket
(60.65~\AA{} away). The same protocol, extended to 14 cryo-EM structures of
the CDK7/cyclin~H/MAT1 CAK complex (a single-target series), found 13/14
failing. As a pipeline sanity check rather than a comprehensive benchmark,
the same pipeline was run on 20 objectively selected CASF-2016 complexes
(8/20 passing, 40\%; exact 95\%~CI~19--64\%), ruling out a broken pipeline.
Neither
nominal resolution nor wwPDB map/model metrics (Q-score, residue inclusion)
predicted redocking outcome; with only two Q-scored passing structures, this
is a descriptive finding, not a definitive statistical correlation. Against
a matched rigid control (4.50$\pm$0.05~\AA{}), allowing five active-site
residues within 4.5~\AA{} of native berzosertib to move improved the 9L40
pose (3.00$\pm$0.92~\AA{}) without reaching threshold. Docking a
40-compound pilot library against excluded 9L40 instead of validated 9L4B
left the top five ranked compounds unchanged but produced low overall rank
concordance (Spearman $\rho=0.21$) and only 5/10 shared top-10 compounds:
receptor choice materially reorders a screen's mid-ranks while preserving
its very top. These results support cognate redocking as a structure-specific
quality-control test whose necessity and sufficiency for prospective
enrichment require systematic, multi-target evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Molecular docking remains one of the most widely used computational methods
in structure-based drug discovery, owing to its favorable balance of
computational speed and predictive utility~\cite{trott2010,hartshorn2007}.
The reliability of docking results depends on a chain of interdependent
choices: receptor structure, ligand preparation, scoring function, search
algorithm, and validation protocol. Among these, the quality and
conformational state of the receptor structure is arguably the most
consequential single factor, since errors introduced at this stage
propagate through every downstream analysis -- a claim this study tests
directly rather than merely asserts.

Cryo-EM has emerged as a powerful technique for determining macromolecular
structures at resolutions that are increasingly competitive with X-ray
crystallography~\cite{pintilie2020,kuhlbrandt2014}. However, the
relationship between nominal resolution and the suitability of a deposited
structure for computational docking is not straightforward: even
medium-resolution cryo-EM structures, cross-docked under a standard
protocol, have been reported to succeed at roughly half the rate of
high-resolution crystal structures~\cite{lee2023}. Several recent studies
have likewise highlighted the importance of per-structure validation in the
context of AlphaFold2-predicted models~\cite{holcomb2023,scardino2022},
suggesting that structure-specific empirical tests may be necessary
regardless of the perceived quality or provenance of the input model.

ATR and CHK1 are established ATP-competitive kinase targets with multiple
chemical inhibitors reported for structure-based
design~\cite{kumagai2006,bass2016,saldivar2017,casper2002,debatisse2012,fokas2012}.
Recent cryo-EM structures of the ATR kinase domain---PDB~9L40 (bound to
berzosertib, 2.87~\AA{}) and PDB~9L4B (bound to camonsertib,
3.20~\AA{})~\cite{wang2025}---provide substantially improved structural
templates for docking compared with the original 4.7~\AA{} ATR--ATRIP complex
(PDB~5YZ0). A companion study revealed that berzosertib additionally engages
a dimer-interface allosteric site~\cite{wang2025}, further underscoring the
structural complexity of this target system. The practical, protocol-level
question this raises is narrower than target biology: a rigid-receptor
docking run treats a deposited cryo-EM coordinate set as ground truth, so if
that single static model does not itself reproduce the experimental ligand
pose it was built from, no amount of downstream scoring sophistication can
recover a physically meaningful result. We therefore asked whether nominal
resolution and standard cryo-EM map-model quality metrics are sufficient to
identify structures that can reproduce their deposited experimental ligand
poses under a fixed docking protocol.

We note that cognate redocking---reproducing a receptor's own bound ligand
pose---is a necessary sanity check for any docking protocol, but is not
itself sufficient to establish prospective screening
performance~\cite{jain2008}. The present study should
be read as a methodological investigation of this validation step rather than
as a comprehensive virtual screening campaign. Docking estimates predicted
binding and cannot establish functional modulation~\cite{kumagai2006,bass2016}.

\section{Materials and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Receptor structures and preparation}

Three receptor structures were obtained in mmCIF format from the RCSB PDB AWS
Open Data mirror (snapshot~2026-01-01): PDB~9L40 (ATR, bound to berzosertib,
2.87~\AA{}), PDB~9L4B (ATR, bound to camonsertib, 3.20~\AA{}), and PDB~2YM8
(human CHK1, 2.07~\AA{})~\cite{wang2025}. 2YM8 is included as a non-cryo-EM
(X-ray crystallographic) kinase reference structure to test whether the
observed behavior is specific to cryo-EM-derived receptors; with only one
such comparator, this test is necessarily limited to $n=1$ and is reported
as such (Limitations). PDB~5YZ0 (4.7~\AA{}) was excluded as
its resolution is insufficient to resolve ATP-pocket side-chain positions
reliably~\cite{wang2025}. Both protein chains (full homodimer) were retained
via Biopython~\cite{cock2009}.

For 9L40, contact analysis (heavy atoms within 4.5~\AA{} of each protein
chain) confirmed residues A2701/B2701 as the ATP-competitive active site and
A2702/B2702 as the dimer-interface allosteric site~\cite{wang2025}; the
60.65~\AA{} centroid-to-centroid distance between the two is nearly three
times the 22~\AA{} docking box used here, ruling out the allosteric site as
a confound for the ATP-pocket redocking result.

\textbf{Receptor preparation} was performed with Open Babel~3.1.1~\cite{oboyle2011}
and Meeko (2025). For each structure:
\begin{itemize}\sloppy
\item Hydrogens were added at pH~7.4 (Open Babel \texttt{--pH 7.4});
\item Alternate locations were resolved by retaining the highest-occupancy
  conformer; no alternate conformers were present among residues within
  5~\AA{} of the cognate ligand in any of the three primary structures;
\item Crystallographic waters and non-ligand heteroatoms were removed;
\item Asp/Glu negative; Lys/Arg positive; histidine tautomer states were
  assigned by Meeko during flexible-receptor preparation, requiring an
  explicit HIE assignment for HIS2477 (Section~2.8);
\item Meeko preparation automatically deleted \textasciitilde400~residues whose
  side-chain density could not be template-matched at approximately
  3~\AA{} resolution; automatically dropping surface/loop side chains that
  cannot be reliably placed in an incomplete cryo-EM map is standard
  practice for receptor preparation at this resolution regime, not a
  study-specific deviation, and all deleted residues were $>$10~\AA{} from
  the docking box (Supplementary Table~S2);
\item Proteins were converted to PDBQT with Open Babel using Gasteiger-Marsili
  partial charges~\cite{gas_marsili} (\texttt{--gen3d --partialcharge
  gasteiger}).
\end{itemize}

Physiological pH~7.4 and Gasteiger-Marsili charges were applied uniformly to
every receptor and ligand in this study (redocking set, pilot library, CAK
survey, and CASF-2016 subset alike) because they are parameter-free,
per-atom defaults that require no target- or ligand-specific tuning, making
them the appropriate choice for a protocol that is deliberately held
constant across a structurally and chemically heterogeneous set of
receptors and compounds rather than optimized for any one of them; a more
sophisticated charge model or per-compound protonation-state prediction
would reintroduce exactly the kind of ligand-specific tuning this study's
design is meant to avoid.

The 22~\AA{} cubic search box was retained as the single, fixed protocol
parameter across all receptors and all 45 library/survey ligands (5 seeds,
35 derivatives, and the CASF/CAK cognate ligands), rather than sized
per-ligand, because every ligand docked in this study is a single-site,
ATP-competitive kinase inhibitor under approximately 550~Da: centered on the
cognate ligand centroid, a 22~\AA{} cube comfortably contains such a ligand
together with its first-shell pocket residues in every structure tested, so
a static box size does not by itself bias the comparison across receptors.
This approach follows the recommendation of Feinstein and
colleagues~\cite{feinstein2021} that search box dimensions should be
justified relative to the specific ligand and receptor rather than chosen
by default; a systematic per-ligand box-size sensitivity sweep across the
full benchmark was not performed and is noted as a limitation
(Section~3.7).

wwPDB validation reports gave per-ligand metrics for the active-site ligand
copy used as the redocking reference in each structure: 9L40/A2701,
Q-score~0.553, residue inclusion~0.818; 9L4B/A2701, Q-score~0.528, residue
inclusion~0.967~\cite{pintilie2020}. These values indicate both deposited
ligand models have comparable local map support, although a comparable
Q-score does not by itself establish the correctness of the modeled
pose~\cite{pintilie2020}.

\subsection{Seed library and derivatives}

Five compounds formed the seed library: the three deposited active-site
ligands (berzosertib, camonsertib, CHK1 inhibitor from 2YM8), plus prexasertib
and ceralasertib (SMILES verified against reported molecular formula and exact
mass). The seed set was fragmented with the BRICS algorithm~\cite{degen2008}
into 22 unique fragments, recombined via RDKit~\cite{rdkit}, capped at 5,000
candidates. Pre-filtering by relaxed Lipinski~\cite{lipinski1997} and
Veber~\cite{veber2002} rules, plus PAINS~\cite{baell2010}/Brenk structural
alert removal~\cite{brenk2008}, left 786 candidates. Retained compounds were
scored against four drug-likeness rules (Lipinski, Veber, Ghose~\cite{ghose1999},
Egan~\cite{egan2000}); 680 of 786 (86.5\%) satisfied $\ge$3/4 rules. The 35
derivatives selected for pilot docking were the highest-ranked by number of
rules satisfied, with ties broken by ascending molecular weight. The complete
ranking and selection scores are in Supplementary Table~S4. This selection
protocol is necessarily biased toward smaller, rule-compliant molecules, a
limitation that should be considered when interpreting the pilot docking
ranking.

\subsection{Redocking validation protocol}

Each receptor was validated by extracting its deposited ligand, redocking into
the identical receptor/box with AutoDock Vina~1.2.7
(Python bindings)~\cite{trott2010} at exhaustiveness~32, three independent
seeded replicates (seeds~1000,~1001,~1002), and computing RMSD (heavy atoms)
against the deposited native pose, with a pre-specified pass threshold of
$\le$2.0~\AA{}~\cite{hartshorn2007}. The threshold was chosen to match the
Astex Diverse Set convention~\cite{hartshorn2007} and was fixed before any
receptor was tested, to avoid post-hoc threshold selection.

Two RMSD variants are reported: naive index-matched RMSD and
symmetry-corrected RMSD (spyrmsd~v0.6.2, graph-isomorphism atom
matching~\cite{meli2020}). The symmetry-corrected RMSD $\mathrm{RMSD_s}$
between a docked pose $P_d$ and the reference pose $P_r$ is defined as:
\[
\mathrm{RMSD_s}(P_d, P_r) = \min_{\sigma \in S} \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N} \| \mathbf{x}_{d,i} - \mathbf{x}_{r,\sigma(i)} \|^2 }
\]
where $\sigma$ is a permutation of equivalent atoms in the set $S$ of
topological symmetries and $N$ is the number of heavy atoms. This correction
is important for ligands such as berzosertib that contain internal topological
symmetries (equivalent ring atoms, sulfone oxygens), for which index-matched
RMSD can overstate the true pose error~\cite{meli2020}.

Search reproducibility $\rho_{\text{repro}}$ was defined as the mean pairwise
RMSD between each replicate's best-scoring pose across the three seeded runs:
\[
\rho_{\text{repro}} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i < j} \mathrm{RMSD}(P_{\text{best},i},
P_{\text{best},j})
\]
This metric characterizes the stochastic variability of the Vina search
rather than its accuracy against the native pose, an important distinction
this study keeps separate throughout (Results).

Both sanity controls passed: crystal-vs-itself returned exactly 0.000~\AA{},
and an exact 1.0~\AA{} rigid translation was recovered as exactly
1.000~\AA{}. A receptor failing the redocking control in any replicate was
excluded from candidate scoring.

\subsection{Ligand preparation}

For cognate redocking, the deposited ligand chemical state (connectivity,
stereochemistry, protonation, tautomer) was preserved unchanged, since the
purpose of that test is to ask whether the docking protocol can recover a
known experimental pose, not to explore alternative protonation states. For
the pilot screen, seed and derivative ligands were 3D-embedded and
protonated at pH~7.4 with Open Babel, then assigned Gasteiger-Marsili
partial charges before conversion to PDBQT -- the same parameter-free
protocol used for every receptor and ligand in this study (Section~2.1),
rather than a more exhaustive per-compound tautomer/protomer search, which
was not run and is not claimed here.

\subsection{Pilot library docking}

The 5 seeds plus the 35 selected derivatives were docked against each
passing receptor using the identical protocol except exhaustiveness~16 (three
seeds: 2000,~2001,~2002). Because the protocol consistency argument requires
validation at screening-level exhaustiveness, redocking was re-run at
exhaustiveness~16: the same pass/fail pattern held (9L40
3.01$\pm$0.06~\AA{} fail, 9L4B~0.59$\pm$0.02~\AA{} pass,
2YM8~0.92$\pm$0.01~\AA{} pass), confirming that the reliability conclusion
is robust to this parameter choice.

\subsection{Multi-structure survey}

The identical validation protocol was applied to 14 additional cryo-EM
structures of the CDK7/cyclin~H/MAT1 CAK complex~\cite{cushing2024}, each
bound to a distinct ATP-competitive inhibitor. These 14 structures derive
from a single target system and a single research group and therefore
represent a clustered, single-target series rather than independent targets.
Because the CAK structures are closely related and derive from a single
target system, the 13/14 result is treated as descriptive evidence of failure
within this series rather than as a population estimate. The pipeline was
verified on 9L4B: it reproduced the original result almost exactly
(RMSD~0.60$\pm$0.03 vs.~0.61$\pm$0.02~\AA{}; Q-score~0.528 identical),
confirming that the automated survey pipeline is consistent with the
hand-curated protocol.

\subsection{Positive-control sanity check}

Twenty CASF-2016~\cite{su2019} complexes were selected by an objective,
non-random rule fixed before any of the 20 was inspected individually: the
first 20 PDB IDs, in file order, of a complete CASF-2016 core-set listing
redistributed, not curated or re-ordered by us, in the \texttt{DeepDock}
GitHub repository.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/OptiMaL-PSE-Lab/DeepDock},
file \texttt{Validation\_Docking/DockingResults\_CASF2016\_CoreSet.csv}.}
This sequential selection rule, rather than a stratified or pseudorandom
draw, was chosen specifically to avoid any appearance of hand-picking
targets favorable to the pipeline. The 285-complex CASF-2016 core
set~\cite{su2019} is a widely used benchmark for docking protocol
validation, and the 58\% Vina self-docking success rate reported for it on
this same benchmark~\cite{zhang2023} is a useful contextual reference.
However, this reference value was obtained under specific preparation and
sampling conditions different from ours, and therefore should not be
interpreted as a universal ``Vina success rate.'' This control is framed
strictly as a
pipeline sanity check -- confirming the pipeline is not badly broken -- and
not as a comprehensive docking-accuracy benchmark: 20 complexes from one
core set, under one fixed generic protocol, cannot substitute for a
systematic accuracy evaluation, and failure to reject a null hypothesis of
equivalence to the 58\% literature figure does not itself establish that
equivalence. Exact binomial 95\% confidence intervals are reported using the
Clopper-Pearson method~\cite{clopper1934}.

\subsection{Flexible-sidechain redocking}

As a test for rigid-conformer contributions to the 9L40 failure, five
active-site residues within 4.5~\AA{} of native berzosertib (chain~A:
LYS2327, TYR2365, GLU2378, TRP2379, HIS2477) were selected \emph{a priori}
from the native complex (not after inspecting redocked poses) and allowed to
move with Vina's flexible-sidechain feature. The 4.5~\AA{} cutoff is the
same heavy-atom contact-shell radius used earlier in this study to
disambiguate the ATP-competitive site from the allosteric site
(Section~2.1); the resulting five-residue set is therefore a direct,
pre-specified consequence of applying that fixed radius to the native pose,
not a count chosen for effect.

Rigid and flexible receptor parts were prepared with Meeko
(\texttt{mk\_prepare\_receptor.py}), which required an explicit histidine
tautomer assignment for HIS2477 (HIE) and, as in Section~2.1, automatically
removed template-unmatched surface residues; none of the five target
flexible residues were among those removed. The docking box was enlarged
from 22~\AA{} (rigid redocking, Section~2.1) to 30~\AA{} to give the
flexible side chains room to explore alternate rotamers without a
ligand-outside-box error. Because this changes three things relative to the
original rigid redocking result at once -- side-chain flexibility, box
size, and the Meeko-prepared receptor composition -- a matched control was
run alongside the flexible run: fully rigid redocking (no residues declared
flexible) with the identical enlarged 30~\AA{} box and the identical
Meeko-prepared receptor and ligand, isolating the effect of flexibility
alone from the other two changes. Both conditions used three seeded
replicates (1000, 1001, 1002, exhaustiveness~32, matching the original
rigid-redocking protocol). Individual replicate RMSDs are in Supplementary
Table~S5.

\subsection{Rank concordance analysis}

The 40-compound pilot set was docked against excluded 9L40 with the same
three seeds used for 9L4B (2000,~2001,~2002) for a matched comparison. Rank
concordance metrics included: Spearman rank correlation $\rho$ with
bootstrap 95\% confidence interval (10,000 resamples), Kendall $\tau$,
mean absolute rank displacement, and top-10 overlap fraction. The bootstrap
procedure resampled compound pairs with replacement to estimate the
uncertainty of $\rho$, accounting for the small set size~\cite{efron1993}.

\subsection{Computational environment and reproducibility}

All calculations: Vina~1.2.7 (Python bindings), Open Babel~3.1.1,
RDKit~2026.03.6, Biopython~1.88, Meeko (2025), all CPU only on a
standard workstation (no GPU acceleration). Exact command-line arguments,
prepared PDBQT files, and individual replicate RMSDs are provided in the
repository. The three seeded RMSD values for each structure are reported
individually in Supplementary Table~S6; reported mean~$\pm$~SD describes
variation among the three docking runs and does not represent inferential
uncertainty about receptor suitability. All software versions, random seeds,
thresholds, and selection criteria were recorded before the first pipeline
execution and are available for independent verification.

\subsection{Multi-structure survey}

Table~2 reports results for all 17 structures tested. Within this sample, 14 of 17 (82\%)
failed the 2.0~\AA{} threshold; only 9L4B, 2YM8, and one CAK entry (8P75)
passed. No statistically significant monotonic association between reported
resolution and redocking outcome was detected (Spearman $\rho=-0.28$,
$p=0.28$, $n=17$). No statistically significant association between the
evaluated global measure of residue inclusion and redocking outcome was
detected ($r=-0.14$, $p=0.61$, $n=16$). Among the 12 structures with a
reported Q-score, the two passing structures had a lower mean Q-score (0.569)
than the 10 failing structures (0.688); with only $n=2$ passing, Q-scored
structures, this group comparison is a descriptive finding, not a definitive
statistical correlation, and should not be read as evidence that Q-score
lacks predictive value.

Across the CAK failures, the docked ligand centroid remained close to its
native centroid (1.1--4.1~\AA{}) despite RMSDs of 5--7~\AA{}, indicating
reorientation within the pocket rather than gross displacement. This pattern
is consistent with orientational degeneracy at the kinase hinge -- where an
ATP-competitive scaffold can occupy more than one plausible binding
orientation -- or with rigid-receptor limitations that prevent the docking
engine from sampling the crystallographically observed binding mode, rather
than with a setup error such as a misplaced box.

The 14 CAK structures derive from a single target system and research group
and therefore represent clustered observations, not 14 independent targets.
Within this single-target series, 13 of 14 structures failed the
prespecified criterion. Because of this clustering, the 13/14 observation
should not be interpreted as an estimate of the underlying failure
probability for kinase structures generally; rather, it represents
descriptive evidence of failure within this particular CAK series.

\subsection{Positive-control sanity check}

The unmodified pipeline passed 8 of 20 (40\%) CASF-2016 subset complexes
selected by the first-20-in-file-order rule described in Methods, an
objective and non-random selection fixed before inspection (Table~3). The
exact 95\% binomial confidence interval (Clopper-Pearson) is 19.1--63.9\%.
This subset is a pipeline sanity check, not a comprehensive
docking-accuracy benchmark, and is read accordingly: because failure to
reject a null hypothesis does not establish equivalence, it is treated as
evidence the pipeline is not badly broken, not as evidence the pipeline is
definitively valid. The observed 40\% rate was not statistically
distinguishable from the literature Vina value of 58\% on the full 285-complex
core set~\cite{zhang2023} (one-sided binomial $p=0.08$), though this
comparison is limited by small sample size and by protocol differences in
software version, receptor preparation, and sampling parameters. The 58\%
reference value represents Vina performance in a specific randomized-ligand
redocking experiment and should not be interpreted as a universal ``Vina
success rate,'' as other CASF-2016 evaluations using different protocols have
reported substantially higher top-pose success rates (Supplementary
Section~S1).

The CAK series (1/14 passed, 7.1\%; exact 95\%~CI 0.2--33.9\%) showed fewer
successful redockings than the CASF sanity-check subset (8/20, 40\%). Fisher's
exact test gave $p=0.037$ for the prespecified one-sided comparison (motivated
by the hypothesis that CAK success would be lower than the CASF control) and
$p=0.050$ for the corresponding two-sided comparison. The CASF sanity check did
not reveal an obvious gross failure of the docking implementation, whereas 13 of
14 structures in the sampled CAK series failed the prespecified criterion.

\subsection{Matched flexible-sidechain analysis}

Allowing the five active-site residues nearest the native berzosertib pose
to move during docking did not recover it: mean symmetry-corrected RMSD
across three seeds was 3.00$\pm$0.92~\AA{} (individual seeds: 4.29, 2.23,
2.49~\AA{}), and no seed passed the 2.0~\AA{} threshold. Naively compared
against the original fully rigid result (2.53~\AA{}, 22~\AA{} box, Open
Babel-prepared ligand), this looks like flexibility made the pose
marginally \emph{worse} -- but that comparison is confounded, since the
flexible run also changes the box size (22~\AA{}$\to$30~\AA{}) and the
receptor preparation (Meeko vs.\ Open Babel) at the same time as
flexibility. The matched control isolates flexibility alone: fully rigid
redocking with the identical enlarged 30~\AA{} box and the identical
Meeko-prepared receptor and ligand, but no residues declared flexible,
gives mean symmetry-corrected RMSD 4.50$\pm$0.05~\AA{} -- \emph{worse} than
either the original rigid result or the flexible result. Against this
correctly matched baseline, allowing the five residues to move improved
the pose from 4.50~\AA{} to 3.00~\AA{}, a real effect in the expected
direction; it was simply not enough to reach the 2.0~\AA{} threshold. The
larger box size and/or the Meeko receptor-preparation differences, not the
choice to keep side chains rigid, account for most of the difference
between the original 2.53~\AA{} result and either Meeko-based result. The
corrected conclusion is narrower than either the original claim or its
opposite: flexibility helps, modestly, exactly as expected if part of the
failure is a rigid-conformer effect -- but it is not enough on its own to
rescue the pose, so a less-supported deposited pose or a conformational
change beyond these five side chains remains at least as plausible an
explanation (Discussion).

\subsection{Error-propagation comparison}

Docking the 40-compound pilot set against the excluded 9L40 instead of
validated 9L4B produced low rank concordance: Spearman $\rho=0.21$ (bootstrap
95\%~CI $-0.19$ to $0.52$, crossing zero), Kendall~$\tau=0.09$, mean absolute
rank displacement~11.7 positions (max~29), and only 5 of 10 compounds shared
between the two top-10 lists (Table~4). The five shared top-10 compounds
were: camonsertib, ceralasertib, deriv\_002, deriv\_003, and deriv\_019 --
the same five compounds that occupied the top five ranks in the 9L4B
ranking. The top of the ranking is therefore stable across receptors: a
campaign selecting only its top five candidates for synthesis would reach
the same set either way. The churn is concentrated in the mid-ranks (roughly
6--40), where score gaps are well below Vina's commonly cited absolute error
against experiment (2--3~kcal/mol); a bootstrap $\rho$ CI that crosses zero
describes substantial mid-rank reshuffling, not a scrambled ranking overall.
The defensible reading is that receptor choice materially reorders the
mid-ranks of this pilot screen while leaving its very top unchanged, and
that the practical consequence depends on how deep into the ranked list a
campaign selects candidates: a top-5 or top-10 selection is comparatively
robust here, while a top-20 or top-30 selection would draw on a materially
different compound set. This is a demonstration of consequence within this
pilot set, not a general enrichment claim, which would require evaluation
against known actives and decoys~\cite{huang2006}.

\subsection{Implications for practice}

Cognate redocking provides a structure-specific quality-control test that can
identify docking failures not apparent from nominal resolution or the
evaluated global map/model metrics. 9L40 and 9L4B were solved in the same
study, yet only the lower-resolution one passed---an inversion that no proxy
metric captured. The observation was reproduced in a separate CAK structure
series, although the CAK structures derive from a single target system and
therefore do not establish target-independent generality. The 13/14 observed
failures in the sampled CAK series should be treated as descriptive evidence
within that series rather than as a kinase-wide failure frequency.

No statistically significant association between the available global
map/model metrics and redocking outcome was detected in this small survey.
Because only two structures with available Q-score measurements passed, the
Q-score group comparison is a descriptive finding, not a definitive
statistical correlation, and should not be generalized beyond this sample.
Skipping the redocking-validation step this paper argues for is not a
neutral shortcut either: the error-propagation comparison above shows that
scoring candidates against an unvalidated receptor materially reorders a
screen's mid-ranks even where its very top is preserved, so the practical
cost of skipping validation depends on how deep into a ranked list a
downstream campaign selects candidates. These results support the use of
per-structure cognate redocking as a quality-control step, while systematic
multi-target evaluation is required to determine its general utility.

\subsection{Limitations}

This is a small pilot ($n=17$). The CAK set is a single-target convenience
sample, not a systematic survey of recent kinase depositions. The global
Q-score analysis is underpowered (2 passing structures with reported
Q-scores). Local density-fit metrics were assessed for 9L40 and 9L4B but a
full per-residue active-site analysis across all 17 structures was not
performed, as such an analysis would require uniform local validation-report
data that were not consistently available for the CAK series. No molecular
dynamics, MM-GBSA, or cross-kinase selectivity panel was run. The BRICS
derivative library is small (5 seeds, 35 derivatives) and biased toward
smaller, rule-compliant molecules; enrichment-based screening metrics cannot
be meaningfully computed at this scale. Most importantly, nothing in this
study establishes functional modulation---only predicted binding and ranking.

\section{Conclusions}

Cognate redocking provides a structure-specific quality-control test that can
identify failures not apparent from nominal resolution or the evaluated
global map/model metrics. Applied to two ATR structures from the same study,
the test passed the lower-resolution one and failed the higher-resolution
one, an inversion not explained by allosteric-site proximity. Within a
single-target CAK structure series, 13 of 14 entries failed the same
criterion. Cognate redocking is a useful structure-specific QC test that can
expose docking failures not obvious from nominal resolution or the
evaluated global map/model metrics, but its necessity and sufficiency for
prospective enrichment require systematic evaluation across a broader,
multi-target benchmark. Skipping this validation step is not a neutral
shortcut: this pilot's own error-propagation comparison shows that scoring
candidates against an unvalidated receptor materially reorders a screen's
mid-ranks, a cost that grows with how deep into the ranking a downstream
campaign selects candidates, even in this pilot where the very top of the
ranking happened to be preserved. A systematic survey across many
independent targets is the natural next step to determine whether the
proportion of failures observed here is representative of recently
deposited kinase structures generally. All code, data, and structures are
openly available for independent verification.
