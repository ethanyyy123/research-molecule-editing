Manuscript Development and Review History
(Related file -- for editorial/reviewer reference only, not for publication)

This document summarizes the iterative development of this manuscript for
the editors' and reviewers' background. It is offered in the interest of
transparency about the manuscript's development process, consistent with
the AI Assistance declaration in the manuscript itself. A full, version-
controlled record (every round, every script, every data file referenced
below) is available in the pipeline repository named in the manuscript's
Data availability section.

Round 1 -- Scope correction
An early outline claimed the pipeline could support statements about
whether a candidate compound activates, inhibits, or modulates ATR/CHK1
function. External review correctly identified that docking, MD, and
MM-GBSA/MM-PBSA calculations cannot support functional-modulation claims,
only predicted binding, ranking, and pose stability. The project's central
claim was lowered accordingly, not padded with more computation. This
distinction is preserved in the current manuscript (Introduction, Scope,
and the repeated "predicted binding... cannot establish functional
modulation" caveat).

Round 2 -- Verifying review claims against primary sources
A second review round raised several specific, checkable claims about
structural biology and benchmarking literature. Rather than accepting or
rejecting these on the reviewer's authority alone, each was independently
verified before being acted on. Real gaps were found and closed this
round: a docking-accuracy validation control was added, an actual (not
just reported) leakage-exclusion filter was implemented for a benchmark
used elsewhere in the broader pipeline, and a naming inconsistency between
MM-GBSA and MM-PBSA was corrected.

Round 3 -- Attacking the central finding directly
A third round attempted to break the central redocking-failure finding
with a symmetry-artifact attack on the RMSD computation, plus checkable
claims about exact resolutions and an exhaustiveness inconsistency, and
flagged two unaddressed alternative explanations: the nearby allosteric
site, and deposited-ligand density quality. All were tested, not merely
answered in text: symmetry-corrected RMSD was added (spyrmsd), the
allosteric site was ruled out by direct centroid-distance measurement, and
wwPDB Q-score/residue-inclusion values were retrieved and reported for
both structures.

Round 4 -- Extending to a real survey
A fourth round proposed extending the two-structure pilot to a larger
survey correlating redocking outcome with wwPDB map-model metrics, adding
a flexible-sidechain control, and reframing the pilot screen as a
demonstration of error propagation. All three were executed for real
(the extension to 14 additional CDK-activating-kinase structures, the
first version of the flexible-sidechain test, and the 9L40-vs-9L4B
ranking comparison); the results are reported in the manuscript as found,
including that the map-model metrics did not predict redocking outcome in
this sample.

Round 5 -- A decisive positive control, two corrections, one withdrawal
A fifth round demanded a positive control to rule out a broken pipeline as
the explanation for the high CAK failure rate, and independently
recomputed a statistical baseline from a cited paper. The demanded control
was run for real: 20 objectively selected CASF-2016 complexes, passing at
a rate statistically indistinguishable from the literature Vina baseline.
This round also caught and forced correction of: (1) an arithmetic error
in the reported CAK fail count; (2) a mischaracterization of two structures
(8P74/8P75) originally described as independent depositions of the same
complex, when their own deposition titles identify them as deliberately
separated conformational classes of one dataset -- this claim was
withdrawn; and (3) a confounded comparison in the flexible-sidechain test,
where box size and receptor preparation had changed alongside flexibility;
a properly matched control was run, reversing the original (incorrect)
reading that flexibility made the pose worse.

Post-round-5 -- A later draft revision introduced unverified content,
caught during final preparation
Before this submission, a further revision of the manuscript (intended to
bring it to what its own header called "accepted-paper rigor level")
introduced several additional analyses -- a box-size sensitivity sweep,
a receptor-preparation comparison table, an alternative histidine-
protonation and ligand-preparation protocol, and a residue-count
sensitivity analysis for the flexible-sidechain test -- along with
specific-looking numerical results for each. During final preparation of
this submission, every quantitative claim in the manuscript was checked
against the underlying scripts and data files in the pipeline repository.
None of the analyses listed above, nor their reported numbers, could be
found in any script or data file: they were not actually run. All were
removed and replaced with the real, underlying data (the matched
flexible-sidechain control at the box size and preparation actually used,
per Round 5 above). A related error was also found and corrected in the
same pass: a description of the CASF-2016 complex-selection procedure as
a "pseudorandom, stratified" draw, when the actual selection script uses
an objective, sequential rule (the first 20 entries, in file order, of an
externally hosted listing) with no random or stratified component
anywhere in the pipeline.

This history is included here, rather than only in the manuscript's brief
AI Assistance declaration, because the editors and reviewers may want the
fuller context: the correction was caught by checking the manuscript
against its own underlying data before submission, not by external
review, and the authors consider that check a normal and necessary part
of manuscript preparation rather than something to omit from the record.
