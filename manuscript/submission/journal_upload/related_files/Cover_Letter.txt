Scientific context and key findings

Structure-based virtual screening campaigns increasingly adopt newly
deposited cryo-EM structures as docking receptors, often on the
assumption that high nominal resolution and deposition recency are
sufficient guarantees of structural reliability. This manuscript tests
that assumption directly, using cognate redocking (reproducing a
receptor's own deposited ligand pose) as the diagnostic.

1. Two 2025 cryo-EM structures of ATR solved in the same study diverged
   sharply on this test: the lower-resolution structure (PDB 9L4B,
   3.20 A) passed a pre-specified 2.0 A symmetry-corrected RMSD
   threshold, while the higher-resolution structure (PDB 9L40, 2.87 A)
   failed -- an inversion not explained by proximity to a nearby
   allosteric site.
2. Extending the identical protocol to 14 additional cryo-EM structures
   of the CDK7/cyclin H/MAT1 CAK complex found 13 of 14 failing the same
   criterion.
3. Because this failure rate is well below AutoDock Vina's documented
   self-docking success rate on curated benchmarks, we ran the
   identical, unmodified pipeline on 20 objectively selected CASF-2016
   complexes as a pipeline sanity check (not a comprehensive benchmark):
   8/20 passed (40%), statistically indistinguishable from the
   literature baseline and arguing against a badly broken pipeline,
   while the CAK failure rate remained a significant outlier against
   this pipeline's own measured baseline (Fisher's exact p = 0.037
   one-sided).
4. Neither nominal resolution nor wwPDB map-model metrics (Q-score,
   residue inclusion) predicted redocking outcome in this sample.
5. A matched flexible-sidechain control showed that allowing five
   active-site residues to move genuinely improved the failing pose
   (4.50 to 3.00 A) without rescuing it -- a corrected, more careful
   finding than an earlier, confounded version of this comparison.
6. Scoring a 40-compound pilot library against the excluded structure
   instead of the validated one left the top five ranked compounds
   unchanged but materially reordered the mid-ranks of the screen
   (Spearman rho = 0.21), demonstrating a concrete consequence of
   skipping this validation step.

Journal scope and format alignment

This manuscript is submitted as a Short Communication: a focused,
methodologically disciplined test of a single validation practice,
directly relevant to the computational chemistry and molecular modeling
community, rather than a comprehensive virtual screening campaign.

- Open science and reproducibility: all code, seed and derivative
  compound tables, redocking validation results, individual replicate
  RMSDs, and prepared PDBQT files are available in public GitHub
  repositories (see Data availability in the manuscript).
- Exclusivity: this manuscript is original, has not been published
  previously, and is not currently under consideration elsewhere.
- AI assistance in preparation: as disclosed in the manuscript's
  Declarations section, the authors used an AI assistant (Claude,
  Anthropic) to help develop and debug the computational pipeline's
  code and to draft and revise sections of this manuscript. All
  AI-assisted content was reviewed and verified by the authors against
  the underlying code and data, and the authors take full
  responsibility for the content of the published article.
