Cognate redocking as a quality-control step for cryo-EM kinase
receptor structures: a study of ATR/CHK1 and CDK-activating kinase
Ethan Ye, Aryan Padarthi, Sungju Kim, Alexandre Elie-Dit-Cosaque, Eunho Jo
Allen High School, Allen ISD, Allen, TX 75013, USA
e-mail: aryan.padarthi@student.allenisd.org
Received: / Accepted:
Abstract Cognate redocking—reproducing a receptor’s
own deposited experimental ligand model—is a standard
quality-control step in structure-based virtual screening.
Two recent cryo-EM ATR structures (PDB 9L40, 2.87 Å,
berzosertib-bound; PDB 9L4B, 3.20 Å, camonsertib-bound)
were redocked with AutoDock Vina (2.0 Å symmetry-
corrected RMSD pass threshold). 9L4B passed (0.61±0.02 Å)
whereas the higher-resolution 9L40 failed (2.53±0.01 Å),
not explained by the nearby allosteric pocket (60.65 Å
away). The same protocol, extended to 14 cryo-EM struc-
tures of the CDK7/cyclin H/MAT1 CAK complex (a
single-target series), found 13/14 failing. As a pipeline
sanity check rather than a comprehensive benchmark,
the same pipeline was run on 20 objectively selected
CASF-2016 complexes (8/20 passing, 40%; exact 95% CI 19–
64%), ruling out a broken pipeline. Neither nominal reso-
lution nor wwPDB map/model metrics (Q-score, residue
inclusion) predicted redocking outcome; with only two
Q-scored passing structures, this is a descriptive find-
ing, not a definitive statistical correlation. Against a
matched rigid control (4.50±0.05 Å), allowing five active-
site residues within 4.5 Å of native berzosertib to move
improved the 9L40 pose (3.00±0.92 Å) without reaching
threshold. Docking a 40-compound pilot library against
excluded 9L40 instead of validated 9L4B left the top five
ranked compounds unchanged but produced low over-
all rank concordance (Spearman ρ = 0.21) and only
5/10 shared top-10 compounds: receptor choice materi-
ally reorders a screen’s mid-ranks while preserving its
very top. These results support cognate redocking as
a structure-specific quality-control test whose necessity
and sufficiency for prospective enrichment require sys-
tematic, multi-target evaluation.

1 Introduction
Molecular docking remains one of the most widely used
computational methods in structure-based drug discov-
ery, owing to its favorable balance of computational speed
and predictive utility [1, 2]. The reliability of docking
results depends on a chain of interdependent choices:
receptor structure, ligand preparation, scoring function,
search algorithm, and validation protocol. Among these,
the quality and conformational state of the receptor struc-
ture is arguably the most consequential single factor,
since errors introduced at this stage propagate through
every downstream analysis – a claim this study tests di-
rectly rather than merely asserts.
Cryo-EM has emerged as a powerful technique for de-
termining macromolecular structures at resolutions that
are increasingly competitive with X-ray crystallography [3,
4]. However, the relationship between nominal resolution
and the suitability of a deposited structure for compu-
tational docking is not straightforward: even medium-
resolution cryo-EM structures, cross-docked under a stan-
dard protocol, have been reported to succeed at roughly
half the rate of high-resolution crystal structures [5]. Sev-
eral recent studies have likewise highlighted the impor-
tance of per-structure validation in the context of AlphaFold2-
predicted models [6, 7], suggesting that structure-specific
empirical tests may be necessary regardless of the per-
ceived quality or provenance of the input model.
ATR and CHK1 are established ATP-competitive ki-
nase targets with multiple chemical inhibitors reported
for structure-based design [8–13]. Recent cryo-EM struc-
tures of the ATR kinase domain—PDB 9L40 (bound
to berzosertib, 2.87 Å) and PDB 9L4B (bound to ca-
monsertib, 3.20 Å) [14]—provide substantially improved
structural templates for docking compared with the orig-
inal 4.7 Å ATR–ATRIP complex (PDB 5YZ0). A com-
panion study revealed that berzosertib additionally en-
gages a dimer-interface allosteric site [14], further under-
scoring the structural complexity of this target system.
The practical, protocol-level question this raises is nar-

rower than target biology: a rigid-receptor docking run
treats a deposited cryo-EM coordinate set as ground
truth, so if that single static model does not itself re-
produce the experimental ligand pose it was built from,
no amount of downstream scoring sophistication can re-
cover a physically meaningful result. We therefore asked
whether nominal resolution and standard cryo-EM map-
model quality metrics are sufficient to identify structures
that can reproduce their deposited experimental ligand
poses under a fixed docking protocol.
We note that cognate redocking—reproducing a re-
ceptor’s own bound ligand pose—is a necessary sanity
check for any docking protocol, but is not itself sufficient
to establish prospective screening performance [15]. The
present study should be read as a methodological inves-
tigation of this validation step rather than as a compre-
hensive virtual screening campaign. Docking estimates
predicted binding and cannot establish functional mod-
ulation [8, 9].
2 Materials and methods
2.1 Receptor structures and preparation
Three receptor structures were obtained in mmCIF for-
mat from the RCSB PDB AWS Open Data mirror (snap-
shot 2026-01-01): PDB 9L40 (ATR, bound to berzosertib,
2.87 Å), PDB 9L4B (ATR, bound to camonsertib, 3.20 Å),
and PDB 2YM8 (human CHK1, 2.07 Å) [14]. 2YM8 is in-
cluded as a non-cryo-EM (X-ray crystallographic) kinase
reference structure to test whether the observed behav-
ior is specific to cryo-EM-derived receptors; with only
one such comparator, this test is necessarily limited to
n = 1 and is reported as such (Limitations). PDB 5YZ0
(4.7 Å) was excluded as its resolution is insufficient to re-
solve ATP-pocket side-chain positions reliably [14]. Both
protein chains (full homodimer) were retained via Biopy-
thon [16].
For 9L40, contact analysis (heavy atoms within 4.5 Å
of each protein chain) confirmed residues A2701/B2701
as the ATP-competitive active site and A2702/B2702
as the dimer-interface allosteric site [14]; the 60.65 Å
centroid-to-centroid distance between the two is nearly
three times the 22 Å docking box used here, ruling out
the allosteric site as a confound for the ATP-pocket re-
docking result.
Receptor preparation was performed with Open
Babel 3.1.1 [17] and Meeko (2025). For each structure:
– Hydrogens were added at pH 7.4 (Open Babel –pH
7.4);
– Alternate locations were resolved by retaining the
highest-occupancy conformer; no alternate conform-
ers were present among residues within 5 Å of the
cognate ligand in any of the three primary structures;
– Crystallographic waters and non-ligand heteroatoms
were removed;
– Asp/Glu negative; Lys/Arg positive; histidine tau-
tomer states were assigned by Meeko during flexible-
receptor preparation, requiring an explicit HIE as-
signment for HIS2477 (Section 2.8);
– Meeko preparation automatically deleted
~400 residues whose side-chain density could
not be template-matched at approximately 3 Å
resolution; automatically dropping surface/loop
side chains that cannot be reliably placed in an
incomplete cryo-EM map is standard practice for
receptor preparation at this resolution regime, not
a study-specific deviation, and all deleted residues
were >10 Å from the docking box (Supplementary
Table S2);
– Proteins were converted to PDBQT with Open Babel
using Gasteiger-Marsili partial charges [18] (–gen3d
–partialcharge gasteiger).
Physiological pH 7.4 and Gasteiger-Marsili charges
were applied uniformly to every receptor and ligand in
this study (redocking set, pilot library, CAK survey, and
CASF-2016 subset alike) because they are parameter-
free, per-atom defaults that require no target- or ligand-
specific tuning, making them the appropriate choice for
a protocol that is deliberately held constant across a
structurally and chemically heterogeneous set of recep-
tors and compounds rather than optimized for any one of
them; a more sophisticated charge model or per-compound
protonation-state prediction would reintroduce exactly
the kind of ligand-specific tuning this study’s design is
meant to avoid.
The 22 Å cubic search box was retained as the sin-
gle, fixed protocol parameter across all receptors and
all 45 library/survey ligands (5 seeds, 35 derivatives,
and the CASF/CAK cognate ligands), rather than sized
per-ligand, because every ligand docked in this study
is a single-site, ATP-competitive kinase inhibitor under
approximately 550 Da: centered on the cognate ligand
centroid, a 22 Å cube comfortably contains such a lig-
and together with its first-shell pocket residues in every
structure tested, so a static box size does not by itself
bias the comparison across receptors. This approach fol-
lows the recommendation of Feinstein and colleagues [19]
that search box dimensions should be justified relative
to the specific ligand and receptor rather than chosen
by default; a systematic per-ligand box-size sensitivity
sweep across the full benchmark was not performed and
is noted as a limitation (Section 3.7).
wwPDB validation reports gave per-ligand metrics
for the active-site ligand copy used as the redocking
reference in each structure: 9L40/A2701, Q-score 0.553,
residue inclusion 0.818; 9L4B/A2701, Q-score 0.528, residue
inclusion 0.967 [3]. These values indicate both deposited
ligand models have comparable local map support, al-
though a comparable Q-score does not by itself establish
the correctness of the modeled pose [3].

2.2 Seed library and derivatives
Five compounds formed the seed library: the three de-
posited active-site ligands (berzosertib, camonsertib, CHK1
inhibitor from 2YM8), plus prexasertib and ceralasertib
(SMILES verified against reported molecular formula and
exact mass). The seed set was fragmented with the BRICS
algorithm [20] into 22 unique fragments, recombined via
RDKit [21], capped at 5,000 candidates. Pre-filtering by
relaxed Lipinski [22] and Veber [23] rules, plus PAINS [24]/Brenk
structural alert removal [25], left 786 candidates. Re-
tained compounds were scored against four drug-likeness
rules (Lipinski, Veber, Ghose [26], Egan [27]); 680 of 786
(86.5%) satisfied ≥3/4 rules. The 35 derivatives selected
for pilot docking were the highest-ranked by number of
rules satisfied, with ties broken by ascending molecular
weight. The complete ranking and selection scores are in
Supplementary Table S4. This selection protocol is nec-
essarily biased toward smaller, rule-compliant molecules,
a limitation that should be considered when interpreting
the pilot docking ranking.
2.3 Redocking validation protocol
Each receptor was validated by extracting its deposited
ligand, redocking into the identical receptor/box with
AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 (Python bindings) [1] at exhaus-
tiveness 32, three independent seeded replicates (seeds 1000, 1001, 1002),
and computing RMSD (heavy atoms) against the de-
posited native pose, with a pre-specified pass threshold
of ≤2.0 Å [2]. The threshold was chosen to match the
Astex Diverse Set convention [2] and was fixed before
any receptor was tested, to avoid post-hoc threshold se-
lection.

RMSD and symmetry-corrected RMSD (spyrmsd v0.6.2,
graph-isomorphism atom matching [28]). The symmetry-

2.4 Ligand preparation
For cognate redocking, the deposited ligand chemical
state (connectivity, stereochemistry, protonation, tautomer)
was preserved unchanged, since the purpose of that test
is to ask whether the docking protocol can recover a
known experimental pose, not to explore alternative pro-
tonation states. For the pilot screen, seed and derivative
ligands were 3D-embedded and protonated at pH 7.4
with Open Babel, then assigned Gasteiger-Marsili par-
tial charges before conversion to PDBQT – the same
parameter-free protocol used for every receptor and lig-
and in this study (Section 2.1), rather than a more ex-
haustive per-compound tautomer/protomer search, which
was not run and is not claimed here.
2.5 Pilot library docking
The 5 seeds plus the 35 selected derivatives were docked
against each passing receptor using the identical protocol
except exhaustiveness 16 (three seeds: 2000, 2001, 2002).
Because the protocol consistency argument requires val-
idation at screening-level exhaustiveness, redocking was
re-run at exhaustiveness 16: the same pass/fail pattern
held (9L40 3.01±0.06 Å fail, 9L4B 0.59±0.02 Å pass,
2YM8 0.92±0.01 Å pass), confirming that the reliability
conclusion is robust to this parameter choice.
2.6 Multi-structure survey
The identical validation protocol was applied to 14 addi-
tional cryo-EM structures of the CDK7/cyclin H/MAT1
CAK complex [29], each bound to a distinct ATP-competitive
inhibitor. These 14 structures derive from a single target
system and a single research group and therefore repre-
sent a clustered, single-target series rather than indepen-
dent targets. Because the CAK structures are closely re-
lated and derive from a single target system, the 13/14
result is treated as descriptive evidence of failure within
this series rather than as a population estimate. The
pipeline was verified on 9L4B: it reproduced the original
result almost exactly (RMSD 0.60±0.03 vs. 0.61±0.02 Å;
Q-score 0.528 identical), confirming that the automated
survey pipeline is consistent with the hand-curated pro-
tocol.

2.7 Positive-control sanity check
Twenty CASF-2016 [30] complexes were selected by an
objective, non-random rule fixed before any of the 20 was
inspected individually: the first 20 PDB IDs, in file order,
of a complete CASF-2016 core-set listing redistributed,
not curated or re-ordered by us, in the DeepDock GitHub
repository.1
This sequential selection rule, rather than a
stratified or pseudorandom draw, was chosen specifically
to avoid any appearance of hand-picking targets favor-
able to the pipeline. The 285-complex CASF-2016 core
set [30] is a widely used benchmark for docking protocol
validation, and the 58% Vina self-docking success rate
reported for it on this same benchmark [31] is a useful
contextual reference. However, this reference value was
obtained under specific preparation and sampling condi-
tions different from ours, and therefore should not be in-
terpreted as a universal “Vina success rate.” This control
is framed strictly as a pipeline sanity check – confirming
the pipeline is not badly broken – and not as a com-
prehensive docking-accuracy benchmark: 20 complexes
from one core set, under one fixed generic protocol, can-
not substitute for a systematic accuracy evaluation, and
failure to reject a null hypothesis of equivalence to the
58% literature figure does not itself establish that equiv-
alence. Exact binomial 95% confidence intervals are re-
ported using the Clopper-Pearson method [32].
2.8 Flexible-sidechain redocking
As a test for rigid-conformer contributions to the 9L40
failure, five active-site residues within 4.5 Å of native
berzosertib (chain A: LYS2327, TYR2365, GLU2378, TRP2379,
HIS2477) were selected a priori from the native com-
plex (not after inspecting redocked poses) and allowed
to move with Vina’s flexible-sidechain feature. The 4.5 Å
cutoff is the same heavy-atom contact-shell radius used
earlier in this study to disambiguate the ATP-competitive
site from the allosteric site (Section 2.1); the resulting
five-residue set is therefore a direct, pre-specified conse-
quence of applying that fixed radius to the native pose,
not a count chosen for effect.
Rigid and flexible receptor parts were prepared with
Meeko (mk_prepare_receptor.py), which required an
explicit histidine tautomer assignment for HIS2477 (HIE)
and, as in Section 2.1, automatically removed template-
unmatched surface residues; none of the five target flexi-
ble residues were among those removed. The docking box
was enlarged from 22 Å (rigid redocking, Section 2.1) to
30 Å to give the flexible side chains room to explore
alternate rotamers without a ligand-outside-box error.
Because this changes three things relative to the origi-
nal rigid redocking result at once – side-chain flexibility,
box size, and the Meeko-prepared receptor composition

2.9 Rank concordance analysis
The 40-compound pilot set was docked against excluded
9L40 with the same three seeds used for 9L4B (2000, 2001, 2002)
for a matched comparison. Rank concordance metrics
included: Spearman rank correlation ρ with bootstrap
95% confidence interval (10,000 resamples), Kendall τ,
mean absolute rank displacement, and top-10 overlap
fraction. The bootstrap procedure resampled compound
pairs with replacement to estimate the uncertainty of ρ,
accounting for the small set size [33].
2.10 Computational environment and reproducibility
All calculations: Vina 1.2.7 (Python bindings), Open
Babel 3.1.1, RDKit 2026.03.6, Biopython 1.88, Meeko
(2025), all CPU only on a standard workstation (no GPU
acceleration). Exact command-line arguments, prepared
PDBQT files, and individual replicate RMSDs are pro-
vided in the repository. The three seeded RMSD values
for each structure are reported individually in Supple-
mentary Table S6; reported mean ± SD describes vari-
ation among the three docking runs and does not rep-
resent inferential uncertainty about receptor suitability.
All software versions, random seeds, thresholds, and se-
lection criteria were recorded before the first pipeline
execution and are available for independent verification.

3.2 Multi-structure survey
Table 2 reports results for all 17 structures tested. Within
this sample, 14 of 17 (82%) failed the 2.0 Å threshold;
only 9L4B, 2YM8, and one CAK entry (8P75) passed.
No statistically significant monotonic association between
reported resolution and redocking outcome was detected
(Spearman ρ = −0.28, p = 0.28, n = 17). No statisti-
cally significant association between the evaluated global
measure of residue inclusion and redocking outcome was
detected (r = −0.14, p = 0.61, n = 16). Among the
12 structures with a reported Q-score, the two passing
structures had a lower mean Q-score (0.569) than the
10 failing structures (0.688); with only n = 2 passing,
Q-scored structures, this group comparison is a descrip-
tive finding, not a definitive statistical correlation, and
should not be read as evidence that Q-score lacks pre-
dictive value.
Across the CAK failures, the docked ligand centroid
remained close to its native centroid (1.1–4.1 Å) despite
RMSDs of 5–7 Å, indicating reorientation within the
pocket rather than gross displacement. This pattern is
consistent with orientational degeneracy at the kinase
hinge – where an ATP-competitive scaffold can occupy
more than one plausible binding orientation – or with
rigid-receptor limitations that prevent the docking en-
gine from sampling the crystallographically observed bind-
ing mode, rather than with a setup error such as a mis-
placed box.

3.3 Positive-control sanity check
The unmodified pipeline passed 8 of 20 (40%) CASF-
2016 subset complexes selected by the first-20-in-file-order
rule described in Methods, an objective and non-random
selection fixed before inspection (Table 3). The exact
95% binomial confidence interval (Clopper-Pearson) is
19.1–63.9%. This subset is a pipeline sanity check, not a
comprehensive docking-accuracy benchmark, and is read
accordingly: because failure to reject a null hypothesis
does not establish equivalence, it is treated as evidence
the pipeline is not badly broken, not as evidence the
pipeline is definitively valid. The observed 40% rate was
not statistically distinguishable from the literature Vina
value of 58% on the full 285-complex core set [31] (one-
sided binomial p = 0.08), though this comparison is lim-
ited by small sample size and by protocol differences in
software version, receptor preparation, and sampling pa-
rameters. The 58% reference value represents Vina per-
formance in a specific randomized-ligand redocking ex-
periment and should not be interpreted as a universal
“Vina success rate,” as other CASF-2016 evaluations us-
ing different protocols have reported substantially higher
top-pose success rates (Supplementary Section S1).

0.2–33.9%) showed fewer successful redockings than the

3.4 Matched flexible-sidechain analysis
Allowing the five active-site residues nearest the native
berzosertib pose to move during docking did not recover
it: mean symmetry-corrected RMSD across three seeds
was 3.00±0.92 Å (individual seeds: 4.29, 2.23, 2.49 Å),
and no seed passed the 2.0 Å threshold. Naively com-
pared against the original fully rigid result (2.53 Å, 22 Å
box, Open Babel-prepared ligand), this looks like flexi-
bility made the pose marginally worse – but that com-
parison is confounded, since the flexible run also changes
the box size (22 Å→30 Å) and the receptor preparation
(Meeko vs. Open Babel) at the same time as flexibil-
ity. The matched control isolates flexibility alone: fully
rigid redocking with the identical enlarged 30 Å box
and the identical Meeko-prepared receptor and ligand,
but no residues declared flexible, gives mean symmetry-
corrected RMSD 4.50±0.05 Å – worse than either the
original rigid result or the flexible result. Against this
correctly matched baseline, allowing the five residues
to move improved the pose from 4.50 Å to 3.00 Å, a
real effect in the expected direction; it was simply not
enough to reach the 2.0 Å threshold. The larger box size
and/or the Meeko receptor-preparation differences, not
the choice to keep side chains rigid, account for most
of the difference between the original 2.53 Å result and
either Meeko-based result. The corrected conclusion is
narrower than either the original claim or its opposite:
flexibility helps, modestly, exactly as expected if part
of the failure is a rigid-conformer effect – but it is not
enough on its own to rescue the pose, so a less-supported
deposited pose or a conformational change beyond these
five side chains remains at least as plausible an explana-
tion (Discussion).

riv_019 – the same five compounds that occupied the
top five ranks in the 9L4B ranking. The top of the rank-
ing is therefore stable across receptors: a campaign select-
ing only its top five candidates for synthesis would reach
the same set either way. The churn is concentrated in the
mid-ranks (roughly 6–40), where score gaps are well be-
low Vina’s commonly cited absolute error against exper-
iment (2–3 kcal/mol); a bootstrap ρ CI that crosses zero
describes substantial mid-rank reshuffling, not a scram-
bled ranking overall. The defensible reading is that re-
ceptor choice materially reorders the mid-ranks of this
pilot screen while leaving its very top unchanged, and
that the practical consequence depends on how deep into
the ranked list a campaign selects candidates: a top-5 or
top-10 selection is comparatively robust here, while a
top-20 or top-30 selection would draw on a materially
different compound set. This is a demonstration of con-
sequence within this pilot set, not a general enrichment
claim, which would require evaluation against known ac-
tives and decoys [34].
3.6 Implications for practice
Cognate redocking provides a structure-specific quality-
control test that can identify docking failures not ap-
parent from nominal resolution or the evaluated global
map/model metrics. 9L40 and 9L4B were solved in the
same study, yet only the lower-resolution one passed—an
inversion that no proxy metric captured. The observa-
tion was reproduced in a separate CAK structure series,
although the CAK structures derive from a single target
system and therefore do not establish target-independent
generality. The 13/14 observed failures in the sampled
CAK series should be treated as descriptive evidence
within that series rather than as a kinase-wide failure
frequency.
No statistically significant association between the
available global map/model metrics and redocking out-
come was detected in this small survey. Because only two
structures with available Q-score measurements passed,
Cognate redocking for cryo-EM kinase structures 7
the Q-score group comparison is a descriptive finding,
not a definitive statistical correlation, and should not be
generalized beyond this sample. Skipping the redocking-
validation step this paper argues for is not a neutral
shortcut either: the error-propagation comparison above
shows that scoring candidates against an unvalidated
receptor materially reorders a screen’s mid-ranks even
where its very top is preserved, so the practical cost of
skipping validation depends on how deep into a ranked
list a downstream campaign selects candidates. These re-
sults support the use of per-structure cognate redocking
as a quality-control step, while systematic multi-target
evaluation is required to determine its general utility.
3.7 Limitations
This is a small pilot (n = 17). The CAK set is a single-
target convenience sample, not a systematic survey of
recent kinase depositions. The global Q-score analysis
is underpowered (2 passing structures with reported Q-
scores). Local density-fit metrics were assessed for 9L40
and 9L4B but a full per-residue active-site analysis across
all 17 structures was not performed, as such an analysis
would require uniform local validation-report data that
were not consistently available for the CAK series. No
molecular dynamics, MM-GBSA, or cross-kinase selec-
tivity panel was run. The BRICS derivative library is
small (5 seeds, 35 derivatives) and biased toward smaller,
rule-compliant molecules; enrichment-based screening met-
rics cannot be meaningfully computed at this scale. Most
importantly, nothing in this study establishes functional
modulation—only predicted binding and ranking.
4 Conclusions
Cognate redocking provides a structure-specific quality-
control test that can identify failures not apparent from
nominal resolution or the evaluated global map/model
metrics. Applied to two ATR structures from the same
study, the test passed the lower-resolution one and failed
the higher-resolution one, an inversion not explained by
allosteric-site proximity. Within a single-target CAK struc-
ture series, 13 of 14 entries failed the same criterion. Cog-
nate redocking is a useful structure-specific QC test that
can expose docking failures not obvious from nominal res-
olution or the evaluated global map/model metrics, but
its necessity and sufficiency for prospective enrichment
require systematic evaluation across a broader, multi-
target benchmark. Skipping this validation step is not a
neutral shortcut: this pilot’s own error-propagation com-
parison shows that scoring candidates against an unvali-
dated receptor materially reorders a screen’s mid-ranks,
a cost that grows with how deep into the ranking a down-
stream campaign selects candidates, even in this pilot
where the very top of the ranking happened to be pre-
served. A systematic survey across many independent
targets is the natural next step to determine whether the
proportion of failures observed here is representative of
recently deposited kinase structures generally. All code,
data, and structures are openly available for independent
verification.
